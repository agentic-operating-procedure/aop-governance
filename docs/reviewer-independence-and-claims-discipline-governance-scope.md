# Reviewer Independence and Claims Discipline (Governance Scope)

Normative for the governance process from its adoption. This document is CR-018: how the estate's definitions, tests and claims are reviewed by people outside the authoring path, how dissent and appeals work, and how public claims are disciplined. The programme owner's decision rights (the change model) are unchanged; this document adds the independence layer on top of them.

## 1. Why this exists

A standard whose definitions, conformance suites and evaluation claims are authored, certified and approved by the same hands is not wrong, but it is not independently credible. This document establishes the independence the estate can run at its current scale: proportionate to a draft standard, and honest about what it cannot yet provide.

## 2. Reviewer eligibility and conflicts of interest

GI-1: A normative specification change receives review from at least one implementer or domain reviewer outside the authoring path: a person who did not write the change, did not author the artefacts it modifies, and does not report to the author.

GI-2: A reviewer declares conflicts of interest at review time: employment, funding, product ownership, or a competing artefact affected by the change. A declared conflict does not always disqualify; it is recorded with the review, and a disqualifying conflict passes the review to another reviewer.

GI-3: Certification and evaluation claims receive an evidence review distinct from the artefact author: a reviewer who checks that the results, cohorts, versions and limitations stated in the claim match the recorded evidence (the reproducibility protocol's artefacts), not that the code is good.

## 3. Quorum, dissent and appeals

GI-4: Normative changes and claim publications require two recorded approvals: the programme owner (whose decision rights stand) and one independent reviewer per GI-1 or GI-3. The independent reviewer's approval covers interpretation and evidence, not the owner's direction.

GI-5: Dissent is recorded, not resolved by force: a reviewer who disagrees records the dissent with the change; the change may land with the dissent attached, and the dissent travels with every claim that depends on it. A dissenting reviewer may escalate to the findings process; an unresolved dispute on a claim narrows the claim until resolved.

GI-6: Appeals: a party affected by a conformance interpretation, a certification decision, or a published evaluation claim may appeal by filing a finding against `aop-governance`. The appeal is decided by the programme owner with at least one independent reviewer who was not party to the original decision. The outcome and its reasoning are recorded.

## 4. Claims discipline

GI-7: Every public claim cites: the artefact version and digest, the profile version, the cohort or dataset, the protocol, the result with its uncertainty, and the known limitations. The reproducibility protocol defines the artefact side; this clause makes citation mandatory on the claim side.

GI-8: A claim invalidated by later findings is corrected or withdrawn: the withdrawal is recorded in the same place the claim was published, states what was wrong, and links the finding. Silence is not a withdrawal.

GI-9: Conformance certificates state what they do not prove (the certification process carries this); evaluation reports carry their cohort label (CR-031's claim classes); adoption material never presents a maturity stage as conformance.

## 5. Stewardship succession

GI-10: The estate documents its stewardship so the standard does not depend on one individual: the programme owner holds decision rights, and the succession plan names the mechanism (delegation of the owner's role, or transfer to a stewardship body) with the estate's own change process as the vehicle. Until a succession event occurs, the owner's decision rights stand unchanged; the plan is the commitment that the mechanism exists.

## 6. What this document does not fix

It does not create an external certification body (the metamodel's rejected candidates note records what would trigger one). It does not make the programme owner's decision rights shared: the owner still decides; the independent layer reviews, dissents, and disciplines claims. Proportionality is the design: two approvals, recorded dissent, one appeal path, cited claims.
